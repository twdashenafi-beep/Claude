What you are waiting on

The work that actually slips is rarely something you forgot to do. It is something somebody else has, and you have lost track of how long they have had it. Owe Me keeps the person's name against the thing, says how long it has been sitting, and writes the message asking for it back — covering everything that person owes you, not one item at a time.

Write it the way you would say it

“Call Mekdi tomorrow at 11” arrives dated, timed and filed. “Owe me the signed lease” goes to the right-hand column with Mekdi's name on it. Hold the button and say it instead, if your hands are full.

Your day, as it actually is

With your permission DayFlow reads your calendar and draws the shape of the day above the list: what is booked, and what is left of it. On a Friday it adds up the week by outcome — what moved, what slipped, who is still holding something of yours.

Nobody can read it, including me

Tasks are encrypted on your device with a key derived from a password that is never sent anywhere. There is no account needed to use the app at all; if you want your tasks on two devices, sync runs through a database you own. No analytics, no advertising, no profile, nothing collected.

That is a design rather than a promise: the password cannot be reset and the data cannot be recovered without it, because there is nothing held anywhere capable of it. The privacy policy says exactly what is stored and what can be read.
