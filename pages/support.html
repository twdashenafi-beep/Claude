I forgot my password

Use your recovery code — the 32-character code shown once when you created your account. On the sign-in screen, choose to recover, enter the code, and set a new password. Your tasks are not re-encrypted and nothing is lost.

If you have lost both the password and the recovery code, the data cannot be recovered by anyone, including us. Nothing on the server can decrypt it. This is the cost of end-to-end encryption and the reason the code is worth keeping somewhere real.

My devices are not syncing

Sync runs on launch and about once a minute. If two devices disagree:

  • Check both are signed in to the same email.
  • Check both used the same password — a different password derives a different key, and tasks will look empty rather than wrong.
  • Check the device is online. Edits made offline sync when it reconnects.

I edited the same task on two devices

The later edit wins. Deleting a task is remembered as a deletion, so a device that was offline at the time will not bring it back.

Making a task repeat

Open the task and choose Daily, Weekly or Monthly under Repeat. Tick it off and the next one appears, carrying the repeat with it. Turn it back to Never and no more appear.

The next date is counted from the date the task was due, not the day you ticked it off, so a Friday job stays on Fridays even when it is done on the Sunday. A task left undone for weeks comes back once, on the next date still ahead, rather than as a pile of missed ones. A monthly task due on the 31st keeps the 31st — February takes the 28th without moving it for good.

Keeping a copy of my tasks

Account → Export a copy. It writes everything — open tasks, finished ones, archived ones, projects, notes and voice recordings — to a single file you keep.

That file is not encrypted, on purpose: a copy you could not open without the password you had lost would not be a copy of anything. Keep it where you would keep a password.

Account → Restore from a copy reads one back. It shows you what it will do before it does anything, and it never removes: it adds what is missing, brings a task forward where the copy holds a newer version, leaves anything you have changed since alone, and brings back what you deleted. Reading the same copy twice does nothing the second time.

Voice notes

Open a task, then hold the round button at the right-hand end of the Notes section and speak. Let go and it stops. To leave a longer note without holding the phone, slide your thumb up while holding — the recording carries on by itself and a tap stops it.

A task holds five notes, each up to a minute. A note that was held too briefly to contain a word is discarded and says so; one too long to store is refused rather than silently truncated. Recordings are encrypted inside the task like everything else, so they survive a reload and reach your other devices.

The day's strip is gone, or empty

The strip above your list is drawn from your calendar, so it is only there when there is a calendar to draw. Open Account and read the Calendar link row — it says what it found, and that one line is the answer.

“No calendar linked” or an invitation to paste a link means there is none on this device. A calendar link is kept on the device you pasted it into: it does not sync to your other devices, and it is not included in an exported copy — so reinstalling the app, or restoring from a copy, brings your tasks back and leaves the calendar behind. Paste the link again.

“none coming up” means the calendar was read and holds nothing in the next three weeks. The rest of that line says which kind of nothing: the date of the last entry in it, which usually means it is the wrong calendar of yours, or that its dates could not be read, which is a fault worth writing to us about. Services that offer one link per calendar will happily give you the link to an old one.

The app looks out of date after an update

DayFlow caches itself so it works offline. Close it fully — swipe it away in the app switcher on iPhone or iPad — and reopen it.

Deleting your account

Account → Delete account. This removes your tasks and your account from the server and the device, permanently and immediately. Your recovery code will not bring it back; there will be nothing left to recover.

Something else

Email support@dayflow.day with what you expected to happen and what happened instead — and one screenshot that answers most of the rest before it is asked.

Open Account and screenshot it twice: once showing the Calendar link row, and once scrolled to the very bottom, where a line gives the version and build number. Between them those say which copy of DayFlow you are running, whether it is the installed app or the web page, and what the calendar last reported — which is most of what any answer depends on. Account → This device adds how much is stored and whether dictation and sound are working here.

Please do not include your password or recovery code. They are not useful to us, we cannot verify them, and nobody should ever ask you for them.
